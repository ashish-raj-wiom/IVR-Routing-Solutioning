Wiom · Product Solution Spec · v1.0 · PM deliverable

CSP IVR Service — Masked-Number Call Routing with PIN-Authorised Bridging

A new capability service that lets CSPs and customers call each other about an active ticket without exposing either party's real mobile number. One bidirectional masked number for both directions, a per-ticket PIN registry that authorises off-app calling (callbacks, redials, calls from new SIMs, multi-ticket disambiguation, colleague-forwarded calls), and a graceful dead-end IVR for every unauthorised case. Replaces today's MN1 / MN2 two-number routing entirely.

Audience: Senior PM · Engineering (csp-execution-platform) · Design · Comms / Ops · Legal Type: PM business-behaviour spec
Companion artefact: Leadership-facing design page.

A2. Context & Problem Statement WHAT

What is expected: Observable problem, affected actor, current behaviour, cost of inaction, governing principle.
QuestionAnswer
What problem does this solve?CSPs and customers frequently need to talk to each other about an active Install / Restore / Pickup ticket, but the existing MN1/MN2 routing only works when both parties initiate from the in-app Call CTA on their registered SIM within a 5-minute window. Every other initiation pattern — callbacks from saved contacts, dialer re-dials, calls from a different SIM, multi-ticket customers, CSPs forwarding the masked number to a colleague — silently drops with no feedback to the caller.
Who experiences it?Field technicians on rooftops who miss the customer's call and try to call back from a different SIM. Customers calling from a relative's phone when their own is unreachable. CSPs who see MN1 in their call log and try to dial back. Customers with multiple active tickets (Restore + Pickup) who have no way to indicate which ticket the call is about. Colleagues taking calls on behalf of the registered CSP user.
What is the current behaviour?Two masked DIDs (MN1 = customer→CSP, MN2 = CSP→customer) backed by the legacy es-ivr-calling-service. Lookup is a short-TTL Redis cache keyed on FROM. On miss → silent drop. No PIN, no dead-end IVR, no missed-call notification. CSPs work around by sharing personal numbers — defeating privacy and blocking audit.
What happens if we don't solve it?Trust erosion (silent drops feel like Wiom doesn't work), wasted technician time (jobs missed because the customer couldn't be reached), avoidable support volume (customers ringing helplines because masked calling failed), and continued personal-number leakage (which undermines the masking platform's whole purpose).
Governing decision / principle"Move fast without breaking trust" — the masked-number contract is foundational to Wiom's privacy posture; it must work in every plausible initiation pattern, not just the narrow in-app happy path. Privacy + reachability are not in trade-off; both must hold.

A3. Objective & Definition of Success WHAT

What is expected: Outcome objective, qualitative success, guardrails, rollback trigger.
FieldValue
Core objectiveEliminate silent call drops in CSP↔customer masked calling so that any initiation pattern by an authorised party connects — without exposing real mobile numbers, and with a graceful fallback for every unauthorised case.
Definition of success (qualitative)A CSP can dial back a missed customer call from any SIM and get through. A customer with two active tickets can disambiguate which one they're calling about. Guardrail principle 1No real mobile number is ever exposed to the counterparty under any initiation pattern. The masking contract is absolute.

Any verified instance of a PIN bridging to the wrong ticket. Sustained backend error rate that pushes Exotel to the Fallback URL on more than 1% of calls.

A6. Business Logic, Invariants & Relationships WHAT

What is expected: Business rules, invariants, business-load-bearing entity relationships, threshold principles.

A6.1 Core business rules

#RuleRationale
BR-01Each active ticket has exactly two PINs — one customer-side, one CSP-side. PINs are issued once at ticket creation; never re-issued for the lifetime of the ticket.Symmetric authorisation. Re-issuing would invalidate copies already held by either party.
BR-02Calling eligibility is tied strictly to active-ticket existence. No call can be bridged on a closed, expired, or pre-booking ticket.Routing surface must align with operational reality. Closed-ticket bridging causes confusion and wastes ops time.
BR-03The OWNING CSP (Owner — partner account holder) is the default executor on every new ticket. The customer-side bridge target points to the Owner until a technician is explicitly delegated. On delegation it points to the technician; on recall it reverts to the Owner.Preserves today's Restore pre-assignment calling. Customer needs to reach someone at the CSP from the moment the ticket exists — not wait for delegation.
BR-04A PIN is recognised only if it matches an active Table 2 row. PINs in cooldown (post ticket-close) never bridge.Protects against stale-PIN cross-pollination — a technician remembering an old PIN must not bridge to a new ticket that happens to be reissued the same value.
BR-05When a recognised CSP enters a PIN, the PIN lookup is scoped to that CSP's organisation only. Cross-CSP PIN guesses are rejected as invalid.Prevents one CSP from accidentally or maliciously reaching another CSP's customer via a forwarded PIN.
BR-06PIN possession equals authorisation. The system does not verify identity beyond PIN possession — a colleague holding a forwarded PIN is treated as authorised.UC 13 (colleague forwarding) is a real CSP operational workflow that the design explicitly supports.
BR-07Every call session terminates with one of three outcomes: bridged (both parties spoke), DNP (recipient didn't pick), or dead-end (caller unauthorised / unrecognised). No call may end silently.Trust contract — caller always knows why a call did or didn't connect.
BR-08Missed-call notifications carry the missed party's own PIN as a callback credential. Notification routing (PN / SMS / WA) is owned by csp-notification-service; IVR's job ends at emitting the domain event.Enables callbacks from any SIM. Single multi-channel routing engine in Wiom.
BR-09The IVR backend always returns HTTP 200 to Exotel webhooks, regardless of internal state. Errors are conveyed via empty destination or via a safe-default Switch Case value.Exotel's behaviour on 5xx is undocumented; returning 200 with a safe fallback keeps the flow predictable.

A6.2 Invariants (must always hold true)

  • INV-01: PIN immutability — a PIN value, once assigned to a Table 2 row, is never modified for the lifetime of that row.
  • INV-02: PIN global uniqueness — at any moment, a PIN value exists in at most one Table 2 row across both active and cooldown sets.
  • INV-03: One active row per (ticket, side) — for any ticket, at most one row with side=customer and at most one row with side=csp are in the active state.
  • INV-04: Allocation tied to candidate existence — Table 2 rows exist for a ticket if and only if the underlying execution candidate is in a non-terminal state.
  • INV-05: Routing target follows executor — the customer-side row's bridge target equals the registered mobile of the candidate's current executor (Owner pre-delegation, Technician post-delegation, Owner after recall).
  • INV-06: Identity tables read-only — IVR never writes to customers, csp_users, or any OS-owned identity table.
  • INV-07: Cooldown PIN never bridges — a PIN whose row is in cooldown is never returned by PIN lookup; entering it produces "invalid".
  • INV-08: Masking contract — at no point in any flow does either party see the other party's real registered mobile number on their dialer / call log.
  • INV-09: IVR always returns 200 — every Exotel-facing endpoint returns HTTP 200 regardless of internal state.
  • INV-10: Missed-call event at-most-once — per CallSid, at most one missed-call domain event is published to the bus, regardless of how many DNP signals fire.
  • INV-11: One Owner per CSP — each CSP organisation has exactly one user with role=owner whose registered_mobile is used as the default routing target.

A6.3 Entity relationships (business-load-bearing only)

#Relationship ruleWhy it matters
REL-01A PIN belongs to exactly one (ticket, side) pair and is invalidated when the ticket reaches a terminal state.Aligns with INV-01, INV-03; prevents orphan PINs.
REL-02A Table 1 active mapping is short-lived (≤ 5 min) and tied to one in-app CTA tap session.Bounds the happy-path bridging surface so cached state cannot bridge a stale session.
REL-03A SIM in sim_inventory belongs to exactly one CSP user; removal soft-deletes (audit retained) and immediately stops being matched for future calls.Audit trail for who could have called from which SIM; removal must not retroactively affect calls already mid-session.
REL-04A masked DID is a Wiom-wide resource provisioned by Exotel; there is exactly one bidirectional DID for v1.Truecaller / VLT verification applies to one number; no pool-picking logic.

A8. Design Guidance WHAT

What is expected: 3-4 sentences of design intent + experience principles.
For the PM: intent at the level of "what experience must this create" — not surface patterns or copy.

A8.1 Design intent

A CSP or customer making a Wiom-mediated call should feel that it just works — the masking is invisible when they're in the happy path, and helpful (not punishing) when they're in a fallback. The PIN should never feel like a security gate they're failing; it should feel like a credential they have. When a call cannot connect, the caller must always know why and have a clear next step, even if that step is "call this helpline." Privacy must be felt in the absence of friction — at no point should either party need to think about why their real number isn't visible.

A8.2 Experience principles

Must-doMust-not-do
Show the customer-side PIN prominently in the SMS / WA, alongside the masked number and a single dial instruction. The PIN is a useful credential, not a chore.Never expose either party's real mobile number on the counterparty's dialer, call log, or notification — under any flow.
Show the CSP-side PIN on the ticket card in the CSP App, prominently enough that a screenshot of the card includes the PIN cleanly (UC 13 colleague-forwarding depends on this).Never ask a caller for a PIN they have no way to find (e.g., asking a customer with no SMS history for a PIN they never received).
On every dead-end, give the caller a number to call AND send that number as an SMS — voice is ephemeral.Never drop a call silently. Every termination is either a bridge, an explicit DNP, or a dead-end greeting with a clear next step.
On every DNP, deliver a missed-call alert through the recipient's preferred channel (PN if app installed, SMS/WA otherwise) and include their own PIN as a callback credential.Never block a call with a PIN prompt on the in-app CTA happy path. The CTA is the no-friction path.
On every reassignment, the bridge target flips silently. The customer feels continuity even though the technician changed under the hood.Never send the customer a new PIN on reassignment. They already have one; it still works.

B14.5 Exotel data-flow rules (10 constraints)

B14.6 App-facing companion API (/api/calls/initiate)

Not Exotel-facing — this is how the CSP App / Customer App / Technician App tells our backend that the in-app Call CTA was tapped. Documented here because it's part of the integration surface (App → IVR → Exotel).

B14.9 What we DO NOT do (defensive list)

  • Never return HTTP 5xx to Exotel. Errors are conveyed via empty destination (Connect endpoints) or via {"select": "unknown"} body (Passthru Sync). Exotel's behaviour on 5xx is undocumented.
  • Never include an action: field in a Connect response (e.g., action: "playback"). Exotel ignores it — flow branching only happens via App Bazaar transition wiring on the three Connect outcomes.
  • Never log raw digits in any audit table. Only attempt_number + outcome go to pin_audit_logs.
  • Never call CleverTap / FCM / Gupshup directly for missed-call alerts. That's csp-notification-service's job after consuming ES_CSPIVR_MISSED_CALL_DETECTED from the bus.
  • Never assume Switch Case branches on HTTP status code. It branches on the {"select": "..."} body string (Rule 5).
  • Never forget to strip('"') from digits before validation. Exotel literally wraps the value in double-quotes (Rule 2).
  • Never set fetch_after_attempt: true on Connect responses. Causes double-billing on DNP (Rule 6).
  • Never initiate an outbound call to Exotel from our backend. The App Bazaar flow drives everything; we only respond to webhooks.
  • Never bridge a call on a closed ticket. Table 2 lookup excludes expires_at IS NOT NULL rows; race conditions resolve to dead-end (BEC-06).
  • Never store the customer's or CSP's PIN in plaintext outside Table 2. The PIN attempt log records outcome only.
