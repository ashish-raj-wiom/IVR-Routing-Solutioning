IVR Call Routing — Proposed Solution

A meaningful share of CSP ↔ customer calls drop today. This is the design that closes the gap.

The problem (in brief)

A meaningful share of CSP ↔ customer calls fail to connect today. The current design only works when the CSP taps the in-app Call CTA from a recognised SIM and dials immediately — every other initiation pattern (callbacks, dialer redials, calls from unregistered SIMs, multi-ticket customers, colleague forwarding) drops silently.

For the full breakdown of today's two-masked-number mechanism and the specific failure modes it produces, see Archives at the bottom.

Constraints — what makes this hard

The conditions of today's call surface the design has to handle:

  • Caller may not be on their registered number. They may be on a secondary SIM, a family phone, or an unregistered device — we can't always identify them by their FROM.
  • A CSP can have many active customers at once. Knowing it's the CSP doesn't tell us which customer they want to reach.
  • Multiple humans may share one CSP App login. A technician (e.g., Rohit) signing into the Owner's CSP App is the canonical case — the dialing identity and the logged-in identity don't match.
  • A customer may have multiple active tickets simultaneously. Restore + Pickup can co-exist (Install is pre-service so it doesn't overlap with the others); the system needs to disambiguate which technician they want to reach.
  • The customer may call back from their dialer with no context. They've seen a missed call on a masked number and dial back — in their mental model, calling back "just works." It doesn't today.
  • A CSP may forward the call to a colleague. Common informal field-ops practice; the colleague has no Wiom app installed and is on a different phone.
  • The persona is voice-first and non-app-engaged. A graceful fallback can't rely on opening an app or reading a screen mid-call.

Objective & framing

At its core, this spec describes an authentication system. Every routing decision is an identity resolution; the tables are the identity store; the PIN is the credential. Failure modes are best understood through this lens — anything that fails is an identity-resolution failure, and the question is whether the failure is graceful or silent.

Four terms used throughout

TermMeaning
Recognised The caller's identity can be resolved — their number is in an active mapping (Table 1), or they hold a valid PIN (Table 2).
Authorised The connection is legitimate — the PIN entered (or the FROM matched) belongs to an active ticket for the party being reached.
Seamless The call connects without the user opening an app, navigating UI, or understanding masked numbers. For the in-app CTA path: zero friction. For the fallback path: friction survivable by a voice-first, non-app field user.
Graceful fallback For unrecognised or unauthorised callers — the user understands why the call did not connect and has a clear, achievable next step. Not a dead-end.

The proposed solution

One bidirectional masked number serves both call directions, backed by a small lookup engine that always tries to identify the caller. When the caller can't be identified from their number alone, an authorisation step (below) takes over.

Three pillars

  1. One bidirectional masked number. Serves both call directions; preserves in-app flows; opens up off-app initiation patterns (callbacks, dialer redials, calls from new SIMs, multi-ticket cases, colleague forwarding).
  2. Authorisation fallback for unrecognised callers. When the caller's number alone can't identify them, an IVR step confirms identity. See Authorization resolution below.
  3. Two small tables. Table 1 for the active call mapping (today's behaviour, kept) and Table 2 for per-ticket credentials (new).

Calling eligibility

Calls in either direction are enabled only when the customer has an active ticket of one of these types:

  • Install — onboarding ticket (booking → installed)
  • Restore — service-restoration ticket (recovery after outage / issue)
  • Pickup — equipment / router pickup ticket

When no active ticket exists for a customer, no Table 1 mapping is written, no PIN is issued, and Table 3 returns nothing. Any inbound dial in that state lands in the dead-end IVR (call centre or trust line, depending on path).

What stays the same

  • The in-app Call CTA still works exactly as today — instant bridge, no PIN, no IVR.
  • Existing telephony provider (Exotel) keeps doing what it does best. We expose two endpoints for the lookup; Exotel does the rest.

What's newly possible

  • Customer who missed a call can dial back from their dialer — IVR asks for the PIN they received via SMS, then bridges to their technician.
  • CSP who switched SIMs, lost the app session, or just prefers their dialer can still place the call by entering their PIN.
  • A CSP's colleague can place the call using the same PIN — no new app install required.

Trust layer — recipient-side pickup (already in place)

Routing the call is only half the job; the recipient still has to pick up. Our masked numbers are whitelisted with Truecaller, so the recipient's phone shows a green verified banner with the Wiom name instead of an unknown number or a "Suspected spam" flag. The new single masked number will reuse one of the two existing (already-whitelisted) MN1 / MN2 numbers, so the verified status carries forward as-is — no fresh whitelisting work, no regression on recipient-side trust. Pre-launch verification: confirm the chosen number is actively whitelisted on Truecaller before go-live — operational check, not an assumption.

Authorization resolution

When the caller's FROM isn't in an active Table 1 mapping, the IVR asks for a 5-digit PIN — the credential. Each ticket has two PINs (one for the customer, one for the CSP); Table 2 looks up PIN → other_party_mobile. Standard double-blind routing pattern used by Ola, Uber, Swiggy.

Where the PIN comes from: the customer receives theirs via SMS / WhatsApp at ticket creation (along with the masked number to dial); the CSP sees theirs on the ticket card in the CSP App. For colleague forwarding (UC04), the CSP forwards the same SMS — it carries everything the colleague needs.

Guardrails: 3-retry cap per call (drops to dead-end IVR); PIN rotates daily; CSP-side lookups are scoped to that CSP's active tickets.

Full mechanism, schemas, retry math, and trade-offs in the technical notes. Underlying telephony: Exotel's Connect Applet with Dynamic URL. Exotel docs →

Assumptions

  • CSP and Customer cannot share the same registered mobile (Wiom's data model rejects dual-role).
  • Calling is disabled once a ticket is closed; no calls outside active Install / Restore / Pickup tickets.
  • No guest access — an unrecognised caller without a valid PIN cannot connect.
  • A valid PIN equals authorisation; we do not verify identity beyond PIN possession. A leaked or shared PIN bypasses authorisation.
  • PIN possession is sufficient regardless of who holds it — colleague forwarding (UC04) is an explicit supported case.
  • PIN delivery is reliable — customer's SMS / WhatsApp reaches them before the call is needed; CSP's ticket card is current.
  • IVR / PIN resolution runs without noticeable delay — backend lookups < 200 ms p95 within Exotel's 5-second budget.
  • Exotel disposition webhooks and CleverTap campaigns trigger reliably (missed-call alerts depend on both).
  • Truecaller whitelisting carries forward for the chosen masked number — verified pre-launch.
  • The customer is voice-first and Bharat-resident; Hindi IVR and SMS are the primary surface.
  • The CSP is app-engaged at ticket level but may be in the field when calling.
  • Either party may miss calls; missed-call notifications are the primary re-engagement mechanism.

Missed-call notifications

Successfully dialling the recipient does not mean the call was picked. Field CSPs are often on rooftops, in cable ducts, or low-signal areas; customers are busy, away, or screening. A missed call without a follow-up is a silent failure — exactly what leadership flagged as the most damaging trust failure mode today.

We close this gap by ingesting Exotel's call-disposition webhook and triggering a CleverTap campaign:

  1. Exotel disposition webhook — Exotel fires a callback for every call with the outcome (answered / no-answer / busy / failed). We listen on this webhook.
  2. Backend → CleverTap event — On any non-answered outcome, the backend fires a typed CleverTap event (e.g. missed_call_CSP_to_customer or missed_call_customer_to_CSP) with the parties involved.
  3. CleverTap campaign → notification — The campaign sends an SMS / push to the party who missed the call:
    • Customer missed → notify customer ("Your technician tried to reach you. Call back on [number]").
    • CSP missed → notify CSP ("Your customer tried to reach you. Call back on [number]").

Dead-end SMS (recipient = the caller who hit the dead-end). When the IVR reaches a dead-end state (no active tech / PIN exhausted), an SMS is also sent to the caller's number with the relevant helpline (call-centre or trust line) — so the caller has the number in writing once the call ends. This is in addition to the voice message, not a replacement.

IVR language: Hindi. All IVR prompts (PIN entry, dead-end messages, any future menus) and all outbound notification copy default to Hindi, in line with Wiom's voice-first Bharat user. Regional-language expansion based on CSP geography will follow. Exact wording — for every IVR prompt, SMS template, missed-call alert, and notification — will be decided by the solutions team.
Comms layer dependencies (must land before launch). All outbound SMS — PIN delivery, PIN rotation, missed-call alerts — must be sent via DLT-registered templates with the "Wiom" sender ID, so recipients see "Wiom" as the sender rather than a generic 11444XXXX phone number. WhatsApp comms require the corresponding WhatsApp BSP setup with approved templates.

Use cases 03 – 06

CSP-side fallback — four distinct problems, one shared resolution

These four scenarios fail for different reasons today but all resolve through the same PIN-based path (UC06 has a fast-lane when Table 1 is still alive). Listed separately so the failure modes stay distinct in conversation; shipped as a single code path.

Use caseToday (why it fails)
UC 03 — CSP calls from an unregistered SIM CSP's SIM isn't in sim_inventory (eSIM, dual-SIM secondary, never logged in from this SIM). The FROM isn't in any active mapping; call drops.
UC 04 — CSP forwards the masked number to a colleague CSP shares the masked number with a colleague on a different phone. The colleague's FROM isn't in any active mapping. Call drops.
UC 05 — CSP calls back on a masked number originally initiated by the customer Customer initiates a call via in-app CTA → CSP misses it → CSP taps the missed-call entry to call back. Today this is the wrong-direction dial — MN1 only routes Customer → CSP — so the callback drops.
UC 06 — CSP dials the Wiom number directly from the dialer (fresh call or re-dial) CSP bypasses the in-app CTA — dials the Wiom number from their dialer, call log, or saved contact. No fresh initiateCall fires, any prior mapping has expired (TTL), and the call drops.

Metrics

Top-line metrics for tracking this build's impact (full event catalog in the technical notes):

  • Connect rate — share of dialled calls that reach the recipient's phone ringing.
  • Direct-connect hit rate — share of calls that resolve via Table 1 (no PIN needed).
  • PIN entry success rate — share of PIN prompts where the caller enters a valid PIN within 3 attempts.
  • PIN abandonment rate — share of PIN prompts where the caller hangs up without entering anything.
  • Missed-call recovery rate — share of missed calls where the missed party calls back (within 24 h).
  • Dead-end exit rate — share of calls that end at the call-centre or trust-line IVR; split by which exit was played.
  • CSP-side vs Customer-side PIN usage — split of which side enters PIN, to size demand and abuse signals.
  • Recipient pickup rate — share of bridged calls that are answered; tracked against Truecaller-recognised vs not.

Archives

How masking works today, and the use-case gap the proposed solution closes.

How it works today — MN1 / MN2

The current implementation uses two separate masked numbers per call session, one per direction:

MN1
Customer → MN1 → CSP
One-way route. Only customer-initiated calls work.
MN2
CSP → MN2 → Customer
One-way route. Only CSP-initiated calls work.

Each is minted via initiateCall when the originating side taps the Call CTA in their app, and bound to a short-lived FROM → TO mapping in the backend. MN1 only knows how to route Customer → CSP; MN2 only the reverse.
