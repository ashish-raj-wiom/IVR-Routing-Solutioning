IVR Call Routing — Proposed Solution

A meaningful share of CSP ↔ customer calls drop today. This is the design that closes the gap.

The problem (in brief)

A meaningful share of CSP ↔ customer calls fail to connect today. The current design only works when the CSP taps the in-app Call CTA from a recognised SIM and dials immediately — every other initiation pattern (callbacks, dialer redials, calls from unregistered SIMs, multi-ticket customers, colleague forwarding) drops silently.

For the full breakdown of today's two-masked-number mechanism and the specific failure modes it produces, see Archives at the bottom.

Objective & framing

At its core, this spec describes an authentication system. Every routing decision is an identity resolution; the three tables are the identity store; the PIN is the credential. Failure modes are best understood through this lens — anything that fails is an identity-resolution failure, and the question is whether the failure is graceful or silent.

Four terms used throughout

TermMeaning
Recognised The caller's identity can be resolved — their number is in an active mapping (Table 1), they are a known customer (Table 3), or they hold a valid PIN (Table 2).
Authorised The connection is legitimate — the CSP is assigned to that customer's active ticket, and the PIN was issued for that ticket.
Seamless The call connects without the user opening an app, navigating UI, or understanding masked numbers. For the in-app CTA path: zero friction. For the fallback path: friction survivable by a voice-first, non-app field user.
Graceful fallback For unrecognised or unauthorised callers — the user understands why the call did not connect and has a clear, achievable next step. Not a dead-end.

Constraints

  • Calling is enabled only on active tickets — Install, Restore, or Pickup. Once a ticket is closed, calls in either direction are no longer routable for that ticket.
  • No guest access — an unrecognised caller without a valid PIN cannot connect a call. The PIN is the credential.
  • Dual-role caller disallowed — the same person cannot be both a customer and a CSP user; Wiom's data model excludes this.
  • PIN retry cap = 3 per call. Three wrong entries route to the dead-end IVR.
  • Single masked number — the new design uses one number for both directions (no MN1 / MN2 split).
  • Customer-side SIM is never captured — only the customer's registered mobile is on file.

Assumptions

Access & authorisation

AssumptionImplication
A valid PIN equals authorisation. Holding the PIN is treated as proof that the caller is the legitimate party for the ticket. We do not verify identity beyond PIN possession. A leaked / shared / observed PIN bypasses authorisation.
PIN issuance assumes successful delivery. When a ticket opens, the system assumes the customer received their PIN via SMS/WhatsApp and the CSP can see theirs in the app. Silent SMS failures leave the customer without a credential and no path forward — comms-layer reliability is a first-class build dependency.
The party holding the PIN is the authorised caller — including colleagues to whom the CSP forwards it. UC02 (colleague forwarding) is an explicit supported case. PIN possession is sufficient authorisation regardless of who holds it. Deliberate usability ↔ security trade-off.

User behaviour & environment

  • The customer is voice-first and Bharat-resident — Hindi IVR and SMS as the primary channel.
  • The CSP is app-engaged at ticket level (sees the PIN on the ticket card) but may be in the field when calling.
  • Both parties may miss calls; missed-call notifications are the primary re-engagement mechanism.
  • The customer's registered mobile is on file and stable for the ticket lifetime.

The proposed solution

Replace the two one-way masked numbers (MN1 / MN2) with one bidirectional masked number, backed by a small lookup engine that always tries to identify the caller — and falls back to a 5-digit PIN when it can't.

Three pillars

  1. One masked number per session, not two. Eliminates MN1 ↔ MN2 confusion and turns the CSP's call log into something they can actually dial back.
  2. PIN fallback for unrecognised callers. When the caller's number isn't in an active mapping, the IVR asks for a short PIN that the CSP received at ticket creation. The PIN unlocks the destination.
  3. Two small tables. Table 1 for the active call mapping (today's behaviour, kept) and Table 2 for per-ticket PINs (new) — each ticket gets two PINs, one per side, each routing to the other party.

Calling eligibility

Calls in either direction are enabled only when the customer has an active ticket of one of these types:

  • Install — onboarding ticket (booking → installed)
  • Restore — service-restoration ticket (recovery after outage / issue)
  • Pickup — equipment / router pickup ticket

When no active ticket exists for a customer, no Table 1 mapping is written, no PIN is issued, and Table 3 returns nothing. Any inbound dial in that state lands in the dead-end IVR (call centre or trust line, depending on path).

What stays the same

  • The in-app Call CTA still works exactly as today — instant bridge, no PIN, no IVR.
  • Existing telephony provider (Exotel) keeps doing what it does best. We expose two endpoints for the lookup; Exotel does the rest.

What's newly possible

  • Customer who missed a call can dial back from their dialer — IVR asks for the PIN they received via SMS, then bridges to their technician.
  • CSP who switched SIMs, lost the app session, or just prefers their dialer can still place the call by entering their PIN.
  • A CSP's colleague can place the call using the same PIN — no new app install required.

Trust layer — recipient-side pickup (already in place)

Routing the call is only half the job; the recipient still has to pick up. Our masked numbers are whitelisted with Truecaller, so the recipient's phone shows a green verified banner with the Wiom name instead of an unknown number or a "Suspected spam" flag. The new single masked number will reuse one of the two existing (already-whitelisted) MN1 / MN2 numbers, so the verified status carries forward as-is — no fresh whitelisting work, no regression on recipient-side trust. Pre-launch verification: confirm the chosen number is actively whitelisted on Truecaller before go-live — operational check, not an assumption.

Authorization resolution

PIN-based fallback is the standard pattern in double-blind calling systems (Ola, Uber, Swiggy). When the caller's FROM isn't already in an active Table 1 mapping, the IVR asks for a short PIN that was pre-shared with the legitimate party. The PIN itself identifies the ticket and the direction — Table 2 looks up PIN → other_party_mobile. Full mechanism in the technical notes.

PIN distribution. Each active ticket gets two PINs — one for the customer (routes to that ticket's technician), one for the CSP (routes to that ticket's customer). Delivery channels are different on each side:

  • Customer-side PIN — sent to the customer's registered mobile via SMS / WhatsApp at ticket creation. The same message carries the masked number to dial, so the customer has both pieces in one place before they ever need to call.
  • CSP-side PIN — shown on the ticket card in the CSP App. No SMS — CSPs are app-engaged at ticket level. The IVR PIN prompt points them to the app ticket card.

PIN format (TBD): either the last 5 digits of the ticket ID, or a randomly generated 5-digit value unique across active tickets. UC02 (forwarding to a colleague): the CSP forwards the same SMS / WhatsApp message directly to the colleague — it carries the PIN, the masked number, and the ticket details (exact format to be specified by the solution team).

PIN retry policy: capped at 3 attempts per call; on the third wrong entry the call drops to the dead-end IVR (trust line). With a 5-digit space (100,000 combos), 3 tries gives a 0.003% brute-force probability per call — sufficient for this surface.

PIN scoping (guardrail). When the caller's FROM is recognised as a CSP (in sim_inventory), the Table 2 lookup is restricted to CSP-side PINs belonging to that CSP's active tickets. A PIN belonging to another CSP's customer is treated as invalid — preventing a CSP from guessing or reaching a customer they aren't tagged to. Customer-side PIN lookups are unscoped (acceptable given the 3-retry cap and per-ticket PIN issuance).

PIN rotation: PINs rotate daily over the ticket lifetime to limit exposure on multi-day jobs. Each rotation triggers a fresh SMS to the Owner / Admin; the previous PIN expires the moment a new one is issued.

Missed-call notifications

Successfully dialling the recipient does not mean the call was picked. Field CSPs are often on rooftops, in cable ducts, or low-signal areas; customers are busy, away, or screening. A missed call without a follow-up is a silent failure — exactly what leadership flagged as the most damaging trust failure mode today.

We close this gap by ingesting Exotel's call-disposition webhook and triggering a CleverTap campaign:

  1. Exotel disposition webhook — Exotel fires a callback for every call with the outcome (answered / no-answer / busy / failed). We listen on this webhook.
  2. Backend → CleverTap event — On any non-answered outcome, the backend fires a typed CleverTap event (e.g. missed_call_CSP_to_customer or missed_call_customer_to_CSP) with the parties involved.
  3. CleverTap campaign → notification — The campaign sends an SMS / push to the party who missed the call:
    • Customer missed → notify customer ("Your technician tried to reach you. Call back on [number]").
    • CSP missed → notify CSP ("Your customer tried to reach you. Call back on [number]").

Dead-end SMS (recipient = the caller who hit the dead-end). When the IVR reaches a dead-end state (no active tech / PIN exhausted), an SMS is also sent to the caller's number with the relevant helpline (call-centre or trust line) — so the caller has the number in writing once the call ends. This is in addition to the voice message, not a replacement.

IVR language: Hindi. All IVR prompts (PIN entry, dead-end messages, any future menus) and all outbound notification copy default to Hindi, in line with Wiom's voice-first Bharat user. Regional-language expansion based on CSP geography will follow. Exact wording — for every IVR prompt, SMS template, missed-call alert, and notification — will be decided by the solutions team.
Comms layer dependencies (must land before launch). All outbound SMS — PIN delivery, PIN rotation, missed-call alerts — must be sent via DLT-registered templates with the "Wiom" sender ID, so recipients see "Wiom" as the sender rather than a generic 11444XXXX phone number. WhatsApp comms require the corresponding WhatsApp BSP setup with approved templates.

Metrics

Top-line metrics for tracking this build's impact (full event catalog in the technical notes):

  • Connect rate — share of dialled calls that reach the recipient's phone ringing.
  • Direct-connect hit rate — share of calls that resolve via Table 1 (no PIN needed).
  • PIN entry success rate — share of PIN prompts where the caller enters a valid PIN within 3 attempts.
  • PIN abandonment rate — share of PIN prompts where the caller hangs up without entering anything.
  • Missed-call recovery rate — share of missed calls where the missed party calls back (within 24 h).
  • Dead-end exit rate — share of calls that end at the call-centre or trust-line IVR; split by which exit was played.
  • CSP-side vs Customer-side PIN usage — split of which side enters PIN, to size demand and abuse signals.
  • Recipient pickup rate — share of bridged calls that are answered; tracked against Truecaller-recognised vs not.

Known limitations (out of scope for this build)

  • Customer dialing back after their ticket is closed — PIN is no longer valid; three wrong attempts route to the dead-end IVR ("Open the app or call 88803 22222") since is_customer still returns true.
  • Dual-role caller (same person both customer and CSP-user) — disallowed by the system.

Why it matters

The CSP ↔ customer call is load-bearing in every install, service ticket, and router recovery. When the call drops the CSP has no fallback — they wait, retry, or escalate; the customer waits with them. Repeated drops translate directly into NPS hits, slot abandonment, and avoidable truck-rolls.

MN1 / MN2 — how masking works today

The current implementation uses two separate masked numbers per call session, one per direction:

MN1
Customer → MN1 → CSP
One-way route. Only customer-initiated calls work.
MN2
CSP → MN2 → Customer
One-way route. Only CSP-initiated calls work.

Each is minted via initiateCall when the originating side taps the Call CTA in their app, and bound to a short-lived FROM → TO mapping in the backend. MN1 only knows how to route Customer → CSP; MN2 only the reverse. The masked number is also recycled across sessions — the same physical number is reused across many pairs over time, so a stale number in someone's call log has no guaranteed meaning later.

Where it breaks today

Failure modeWhat happens today
Unregistered / undetected SIM (CSP side) On the CSP side, SIM capture at app login works ~85% of the time. The remaining ~15% (eSIM, dual-SIM secondary slot, READ_PHONE_STATE denied) produce a FROM that isn't in any active mapping. Call drops. (Customer side has no SIM capture at all — only the registered mobile is known, so any call from a different SIM falls through.)
Wrong-direction dial (MN1 ↔ MN2 confusion) CSP sees MN1 in their call log (the number a customer used to call them) and dials it back. MN1 only routes Customer → CSP, so the CSP's FROM isn't in the mapping. Call drops.
Native dialer / call-log redial CSP or customer dials the masked number from the phone dialer without a fresh CTA. No initiateCall fires; the mapping has expired (TTL) or never existed for this direction. Call drops.
Customer callback Customer sees a missed call from the masked number and calls back. No live mapping for the customer's FROM. Call drops.
Forwarded masked number CSP shares the masked number with a colleague on chat. Colleague dials from a phone whose SIM isn't in sim_inventory → no mapping. Call drops.
Telemetry gap None of the above failures emit a distinct event. We can't size which bucket is biggest, so any fix today is unprioritised.

Common thread: every failure mode is rooted in the in-app + registered-SIM + fresh-TTL triple. Anything that breaks even one of those three drops the call.
