CSP IVR Service — Cohort-Based Rollout Plan

Roll out IVR 2.0 (masked-number bridging) on a cohort of CSPs instead of all-or-nothing. The IVR service maintains a list of enrolled CSPs. When the app fetches the number to dial at the moment the user taps the call CTA, the IVR returns the masked number (for enrolled CSPs) or the other party's actual number (for everyone else). Apps stay neutral — they dial whatever they're given. PIN is fetched separately well before the CTA tap, and is unchanged by this rollout.

Audience: Engineering (csp-execution-platform, App teams) · Ops · Design Type: PM rollout-plan spec
Companion artefact: Base Product Solution Spec (v1.0). This document layers a phased rollout strategy on top of that spec; it does not modify the core IVR behaviour.

Quick Check

7-line summary. Reflects the body below.
What problem?
An all-CSP rollout of IVR 2.0 is too risky — we need a controlled cohort first to learn before expanding. Today's app-side kill switches are global ON/OFF; they cannot scope behaviour to specific CSPs.
Primary owner?
IVR service team. The cohort list lives inside the IVR service.
Migration needed?
No. App kill switches remain ON. No backfill of historical tickets. PIN behaviour for the app is unchanged.
App release needed?
Yes — minor. Apps add a single branch when receiving the number to dial: if it's a masked number, use the existing IVR flow; if it's a regular number, dial it directly.
Money flow change?
No.
PII processed?
Yes — same classes as today (CSP / customer mobiles). No new class.
Rollback latency?
Adding or removing a CSP from the cohort takes effect across the IVR service within seconds — no app release, no service restart.

A2. Context & Problem Statement WHAT

What is expected: Why a cohort rollout is needed; what today's kill switch can and can't do; cost of skipping cohort phase.
QuestionAnswer
What is the current state?The base IVR 2.0 spec is built and partially live on prod. Both the CSP App and Customer App have a binary "IVR kill switch": when ON, the app calls the IVR service to fetch the masked-number routing target; when OFF, the app dials the other party's actual mobile directly. The kill switch is global — it affects every user simultaneously.
What can't the kill switch do?Scope behaviour to a subset. Either everyone gets masking or nobody does. Day-1 mass enablement carries every regression risk to every user at once, with no incremental learning.
Why is a cohort rollout needed?The IVR end-to-end flow involves: Exotel webhook timing, App-Bazaar Switch Case wiring, PIN allocation correctness, PIN-fetch endpoint correctness, Customer Service deserialization, sim_inventory recognition, and CleverTap notification path. Bugs in any of these silently break customer ↔ CSP communication. Recent prod incidents (CustomerClient deserialization bug, P74 PIN deallocation leak) confirm that hidden defects only surface once real traffic hits the system. We need a manageable blast radius to identify and fix these as they emerge.
What does this cohort plan add?A per-CSP enrolment list inside the IVR service that decides — on the number-fetch call at CTA time — whether to return the masked DID (cohort path) or the other party's actual mobile (non-cohort path). Apps stay agnostic; they just dial whatever the IVR returns.
Relationship to PIN fetch?The PIN-fetch endpoints (/api/v1/ivr/pin-registry/customer/pin and /csp/pin) are NOT touched by this rollout. Apps pre-fetch the PIN earlier in the ticket lifecycle (well before the call CTA is tapped), and continue to do so for all CSPs regardless of enrolment state. The PIN sits unused on the app for non-enrolled CSPs — harmless, since the number-fetch at CTA time will return DIRECT and skip the masked-bridge path entirely.
What happens if we skip this and just turn on the kill switch for everyone?Any defect would manifest as failed customer ↔ CSP calls across the entire base simultaneously. Reputational and operational cost of a full-base regression is materially higher than the engineering cost of building the cohort gate (~2 days of engineering work).
Governing principle"Crawl, walk, run" — prove correctness at small scale before exposing all users. The kill switch alone is insufficient because rolling back wholesale loses all the partial signal we've gained.

A3. Objective & Definition of Success WHAT

FieldValue
Core objectiveRoll IVR 2.0 to all CSPs with high confidence by progressing through size-bounded cohorts, learning from each cohort, and only expanding when metrics + qualitative signal both clear.
Definition of success (qualitative)Every CSP in the enrolled cohort experiences working masked calling in both directions. CSPs outside the cohort continue to call the other party directly, unchanged. Issues discovered in a cohort are fixed before expanding to the next cohort.
Quantitative success bars (per cohort)Bridge rate ≥ 85% of v2 calls (i.e., to_number set in user_connection_call_logs); Connect rate ≥ 30% of v2 calls; PIN-fetch error rate < 1% of calls; zero "masked number was actual number" incidents (privacy leakage).
Guardrail 1No CSP outside the enrolled cohort sees any change in behaviour. Their tickets continue to expose the other party's mobile via the existing call-CTA contract.
Guardrail 2A CSP can be removed from the cohort within ≤ 30 seconds (one cache cycle). No code change or service restart.
Guardrail 3Privacy invariant from the base spec is preserved: a real mobile number is never exposed to the counterparty for an enrolled CSP's tickets. Cohort gating does not weaken this contract; it only controls who is in the masking regime.
Rollback triggerAny of: (a) bridge rate drops below 70% across the cohort sustained over 24h; (b) any verified privacy leak (real number returned to counterparty when it shouldn't be); (c) a new bug class that affects ≥ 5 distinct CSPs in the cohort.

A4. Rollout Principles WHAT

What is expected: The four cornerstone decisions that govern this rollout.
DecisionChoiceWhy
Where does the cohort list live?Inside the IVR service. Not in apps, not in any other service.Apps stay simple. Other teams don't need to know. Single source of truth. Changes are made by the IVR team only.
Granularity?Per CSP, not per ticket. If a CSP is in the cohort, every one of their tickets (Install, Restore, NBREC) uses masked routing.Simpler mental model. CSPs are the relationship; tickets are transient. Avoids inconsistent UX where one ticket masks and another doesn't.
What decides masked vs. direct?The IVR service decides at the moment the app asks for a number to dial. If the ticket's CSP is in the cohort, IVR returns the masked number; otherwise the actual number of the other party. Apps don't need to know who's in the cohort.Minimum change to the apps. Single decision point, in one service. The cohort can grow or shrink without any app release.
PIN behaviour for non-cohort CSPs?Unchanged. A PIN is still created in the background for every Install / Restore / NBREC ticket, and the app still pre-fetches it. For non-cohort CSPs it just sits unused.Decouples the cohort decision from upstream services. They keep working as today. The unused PINs cost almost nothing.
Inbound helpline behaviour?Bridge anyone. The IVR helpline number accepts all callers; the cohort decision only applies when the app fetches a number to dial.Customers who somehow reach the helpline (old SMS, saved contact) shouldn't be cut off. The helpline is shared infrastructure.
What about a CSP's in-flight tickets when they join the cohort?No backfill. Going forward from the moment the CSP joins the cohort, every call CTA on every one of their tickets (including ones created earlier) will return the masked number. No retroactive change to the tickets themselves.Simplest. The cohort decision is made fresh on every call CTA, so adding a CSP is enough — nothing has to be re-stamped on existing tickets.

A6. Business Logic & Invariants WHAT

IDInvariant
INV-ROLL-01The IVR service is the sole owner of the cohort list. No other service or app holds its own copy.
INV-ROLL-02Cohort membership is a CSP-level fact. If a CSP is in the cohort, every one of their tickets (Install, Restore, NBREC) is in the masking regime.
INV-ROLL-03The IVR returns one of two outcomes when the app asks for a number to dial: the masked number (for cohort CSPs) or the other party's actual number (for everyone else). There is no third outcome.
INV-ROLL-04When the IVR returns the actual number of the other party, it uses the mobile that's already on file from when the ticket was created. No extra lookup at the moment of the call.
INV-ROLL-05PIN creation and PIN pre-fetch by the app are uniform across all CSPs — cohort or not. For non-cohort CSPs the PIN simply isn't used.
INV-ROLL-06Adding or removing a CSP from the cohort takes effect across the IVR service within seconds, without an app release and without a service restart.
INV-ROLL-07The base IVR's privacy guarantees (no real mobile exposed when the call goes through the masked bridge; PIN is bound to one ticket only) are unaffected. The cohort decision is an additional layer on top, not a relaxation.

Part B

HOW — Detailing

Engineering-facing detail. Subject to revision as build progresses; the WHAT decisions above are the stable contract.

B1. What the IVR Service Does HOW

What is expected: The business-level behaviour the IVR service needs to support to enable cohort-based rollout.

B1.1 The cohort list

The IVR service holds a list of CSPs that are currently in the masking regime. The list is maintained by the IVR team; nobody else has direct access. A CSP can be added or removed at any time, and the change takes effect across the IVR service within seconds — no app release, no service restart, no coordination with other teams.

B1.2 The single decision the IVR makes

Every time the app asks the IVR for a number to dial at the moment the user taps the call CTA, the IVR does one check: is the ticket's CSP in the cohort list?

  • Yes → return the masked number. The app dials the masked number; the call goes through the IVR routing layer and gets bridged to the other party.
  • No → return the other party's actual mobile number. The app dials it directly; the call goes through as a normal phone call. The IVR is not involved in routing.

This is the only place the cohort decision is consulted in the entire system. Everything else — PIN creation, PIN pre-fetch, inbound calls to the helpline number, allocation triggers from upstream services — continues to behave identically for cohort and non-cohort CSPs.

B1.3 Where the IVR gets the actual number for non-cohort CSPs

Both parties' mobile numbers are already known to the IVR at ticket creation time (the upstream service that creates the ticket also tells the IVR who the customer and CSP are). So when a non-cohort CSP's ticket triggers a number-fetch, the IVR can return the right "actual" number immediately, without calling any other service in the middle of the call CTA flow.

B1.4 Managing the cohort list

The IVR team needs to be able to: add a CSP to the cohort, remove a CSP from the cohort, and view the current list and recent changes. These operations are internal-only (no public surface) and happen at human-managed frequency (a few times a day during early rollout; less often later).

B1.5 What is NOT changing in the IVR service

  • PIN creation logic for new tickets.
  • PIN-fetch endpoints used by apps to pre-fetch the PIN earlier in the ticket lifecycle.
  • Inbound call routing on the helpline number (callers always reach a bridge as today).
  • The masked DID configuration, PIN registry, sim inventory.
  • Privacy guarantees: when a call does go through the masked bridge, no real mobile is exposed to the counterparty.

B2. Inbound Helpline Behaviour HOW

The IVR helpline number accepts calls from everyone, exactly as today. The cohort decision does not apply to inbound calls.

Reason: the helpline is shared infrastructure. Customers and CSPs who happen to dial it (from an old SMS, a saved contact, a missed-call callback) shouldn't be cut off mid-flow. Filtering at the helpline would create silent drops with no clear recovery path — the exact problem the base IVR was built to eliminate.

B3. PIN Behaviour HOW

PINs are still created in the background for every Install / Restore / NBREC ticket when it gets created, regardless of whether the CSP is in the cohort. The app still pre-fetches the PIN when the ticket loads on screen, regardless of cohort state.

What changes is whether the PIN gets used:

  • If the CSP is in the cohort, the call goes through the masked bridge and the pre-fetched PIN is sent on the call as DTMF, as today.
  • If the CSP is not in the cohort, the call is dialled directly and the pre-fetched PIN simply sits on the app, unused. No harm done.

This means: when a CSP moves into the cohort, their already-in-flight tickets automatically start using the masked bridge on the very next call CTA, because the PIN is already there. No backfill, no rebuild step.

B4. App-Side Changes HOW

What is expected: The minimal change in both Customer App and CSP App / Technician App.
ChangeDetail
Kill switch stateRemains ON permanently. Apps always call the IVR for the number to dial. No code removal; the kill switch is just no longer the meaningful control.
PIN-fetch pathUnchanged. Apps continue to pre-fetch the PIN when the ticket loads and keep it on the ticket card. Same behaviour for cohort and non-cohort CSPs.
Behaviour at call CTA tapWhen the IVR responds with a masked number, the app dials it and sends the locally-stored PIN as DTMF (today's masked-bridge flow). When the IVR responds with a regular number, the app dials it directly and ignores the locally-stored PIN.
Backward compatibility on rollout dayOld app versions continue to work. For cohort CSPs they read the masked number as today and dial it — masking works. For non-cohort CSPs they fall through to the same direct-dial branch they were using before this rollout. No crash mode, no broken state.
Release sequencingShip the app update at the start of Phase 0 (dry run). Reach a comfortable adoption level on both apps before enrolling any CSP whose users may still be on older app versions.

B8.1 Selection criteria for Pilot & Phase 2 cohorts

  • Active ticket flow in at least 2 of {Install, Restore, NBREC} — to exercise all PIN-allocation paths.
  • At least 2 distinct zones represented — to catch any region-specific issues (telco-routing, masked-DID coverage).
  • Reachable for direct PM / Ops feedback (WhatsApp, calls, in-person if possible).
  • Not currently in any other product experiment that could confound signal.

B5.2 Promotion gate from Phase N to Phase N+1

All of: (a) success criteria above met; (b) no open P0 / P1 bugs from previous phase; (c) explicit PM + Tech Lead sign-off; (d) at least 48 hours of clean data since last change.

B6. Metrics & Observability HOW

What is expected: What gets measured per phase to decide whether to expand or hold.

B6.1 Quantitative metrics (daily report per cohort)

MetricTarget
Bridge rate — share of calls from cohort CSPs that successfully reached a masked bridge attempt≥ 85%
Connect rate — share of cohort calls where the bridge actually connected and a real conversation happened≥ 30%
PIN-fetch error rate on the app< 1% of all CTA flows
Cohort decision correctness — every call from a cohort CSP must take the masked path; every non-cohort call must take the direct path100% (any miss is a bug)

B6.2 Qualitative signal (per phase)

  • CSP feedback: phone / WhatsApp check-ins with each Phase 1 / Phase 2 CSP at day 3 and day 7.
  • Customer complaint count from CC tickets tagged "calling issue".
  • Internal escalation log (Slack / JIRA) — count and severity of issues raised.

B6.3 Privacy regression detection

A nightly check inspects a sample of cohort calls and confirms that the numbers exchanged between the two parties were the Wiom masked number, not their personal mobiles. Any case where a personal mobile shows up on a cohort call triggers an alert.

B8. Out of Scope HOW

  • Legacy customer IVR helpline — continues to operate in parallel. Not affected by this rollout.
  • Legacy CSP-app outbound calling path — older CSP app versions that use the legacy call-setup flow continue to work as today, irrespective of cohort state.
  • Customer-side cohort logic — there is no customer-level cohort list. A customer's experience is decided by whether the CSP on their ticket is in the cohort.
  • Notification gating — the SMS / push notifications for PIN delivery are unaffected. Customers and CSPs continue to receive them as today, regardless of cohort state. The PIN simply doesn't get used for non-cohort CSPs.
  • Migrated tickets that pre-date the IVR 2.0 launch and have no PIN in the system — flagged as OQ-1; resolved before Phase 1 kickoff.
  • Admin UI for managing the cohort — not built in this phase. The IVR team makes changes directly until volume warrants tooling.
